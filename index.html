The Californian Condor is a noble bird that was once critically endangered in the 1980s, with less than 20 reported living members, their numbers ravaged due to habitat loss and lead poisoning in the local prey. But due to impressive breeding and conservation efforts by the Los Angeles Zoo and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, this once near exinct bird is making an inspring comeback in both in both the wild and captivity.

U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service's yearly California Condor Population Report , is the source for this data.

Through adding zooming features to the bar graph format, this data display seeks to achieve both being able to display exponential changes over time alongside the ability to focus in on, and get more exact counts from specific points of interest in the timeline.
